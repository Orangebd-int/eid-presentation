Section 02 · Part 1 of 2

Recap: The SXM eID Platform

The eID Platform is the trusted Identity Provider the SSO Platform is built on top of — it never issues identity on its own.

Role in the ecosystem

  • Acts as the government's single Identity Provider (IDP), issuing verified, trusted digital identity for citizens and businesses.
  • Civil Registry remains the authoritative source of truth; the eID Platform never stores an independent copy of identity facts.
  • Identity data flows from Civil Registry to the eID Platform through the Key2DataDistributie exchange system.
  • The SSO Platform consumes eID as its Identity Store — it does not re-implement identity verification.

Section 02 · Part 2 of 2

Recap: What the eID Platform Provides

Four capabilities the SSO Platform relies on for every login and every data exchange.

CAPABILITY / A

🪪 eID Authentication

Verifies the individual is who they claim to be, independent of which government application initiated the request.

CAPABILITY / B

#️⃣ UIN — Unique Identity Number

A single persistent identifier used consistently across every government digital service.

CAPABILITY / C

🗂️ Identity Attributes

Structured citizen/business attributes (name, status, entitlements) shared with SSO under agreed data-sharing scopes.

CAPABILITY / D

✅ Verified Identity

An assurance level the SSO Platform can trust when issuing tokens to relying-party applications.

Section 04 · LLD 1 of 3

Authentication & MFA Flow

Login Journey

How a citizen moves from "I want to log in" to a valid session at a government service.

1
Access Channel
Citizen opens a service via Web, Mobile, PSC or Call Centre
2
SSO Login
Service redirects to SSO Platform's login page
3
eID Federation
SSO redirects to eID Platform for identity verification
4
MFA Challenge
Risk-based step-up authentication where required
5
Token Issued
SSO issues an OAuth2/OIDC token bound to the UIN
6
Access Granted
Citizen lands in the requested government service
Trigger
Unauthenticated request to any relying-party application.
Key Decision
MFA method(s) and step-up rules — confirmed in Open Questions.
Outcome
Single session usable across all federated government services.

Section 04 · LLD 3 of 3

Relying Party Integration

SSO → Applications

The standard, repeatable way any government digital service plugs into the SSO Platform.

1
Register App
Service is onboarded as a relying party in the SSO Platform
2
Choose Protocol
OAuth 2.0, OpenID Connect, SAML 2.0 or SCIM
3
Access Gateway
Secure, policy-enforced entry point for the service
4
Session & Token
SSO manages session lifecycle and token validation
5
Monitor & Audit
All access logged for analytics and compliance
Standards
OAuth 2.0 / OpenID Connect / SAML 2.0 / SCIM — no custom protocols.
Key Decision
Which services form the Phase 1 integration cohort.
Outcome
Consistent onboarding pattern for every current and future service.
